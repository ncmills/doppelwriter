A reference letter is only worth something if it sounds like a real person who actually knows the candidate. AI can help you get a draft on the page, but the specifics and the voice still have to come from you. Here's what a good letter needs, and how to use AI to draft one that still sounds like you.

## What should a reference letter include?

**A good reference letter covers how you know the person, how long you've known them, specific examples of their character, and a clear statement of endorsement.** That's the checklist [DoppelWriter's own reference letter page](https://doppelwriter.com/write/reference-letter) lays out, and it lines up with other writing guides.

Two more details come up repeatedly:

- **Your standing.** [Grammarly](https://www.grammarly.com/blog/business-writing/reference-letter/) says to include your position, your relationship to the applicant, how long you've known them, and your contact information (email and phone).
- **An opening that orients the reader.** [Proofed](https://proofed.com/writing-tips/how-to-structure-a-reference-letter-or-letter-of-recommendation/) describes a standard letter as opening with who you are, how you know the candidate and for how long, and the position they're applying for, followed by two to three concise body paragraphs on why they suit the role.

## What's the difference between a reference letter and a recommendation letter?

**A reference letter is a general endorsement of someone's character, knowledge and skills; a recommendation letter supports one specific scholarship, program or job.** That distinction comes from [Chron, citing Brandeis University](https://work.chron.com/difference-between-reference-recommendation-letter-26700.html).

It shows up in the greeting, too. Because reference letters are often general rather than addressed to one requestor, [Lawpath](https://lawpath.com.au/blog/recommendation-letters-and-references-letters) notes they commonly begin "To Whom It May Concern" or "Dear Sir/Madam". A recommendation letter, aimed at a particular opening, usually names the program or role and the person reading it.

Why this matters for AI: the more targeted the letter, the more specific detail you need to hand the tool. A general reference can lean on character; a recommendation has to argue fit.

## Why do specific examples beat superlatives?

**Because praise without evidence reads as insincere.** [SEEK](https://nz.seek.com/career-advice/article/how-to-write-a-reference-letter) advises being honest without hyperbole ("best, smartest, most amazing") and including an example to justify each quality you name.

Here is how that plays out. The second example below is invented purely for illustration:

| Generic wording | Specific wording (hypothetical example) |
|---|---|
| "Maria is an exceptional, hardworking team player who consistently exceeds expectations." | "Hypothetical example: When our reporting system failed the night before a client deadline, Maria rebuilt the report by hand and had it in the client's inbox by 7 a.m." |

The second version is shorter on adjectives and longer on evidence. That's what makes a letter believable, and it's why the details you supply matter more than the tool you use.

## How do I make an AI-generated reference letter sound credible?

**Give the AI real specifics and real samples of your own writing, then cut anything you can't personally vouch for.** Specifics supply the examples SEEK says every quality needs; your writing samples supply the voice.

DoppelWriter's [reference letter page](https://doppelwriter.com/write/reference-letter) says it learns your voice from your writing samples and drafts the letter in your natural style, and its [homepage](https://doppelwriter.com/) says it analyzes style across 30+ dimensions (sentence length, vocabulary, punctuation habits, tone, rhythm) from 3-5 uploaded samples. It's free to try.

What to bring with you:

- How you know the person and for how long
- One concrete story from your own experience, with the outcome
- One or two honest observations about their character
- A few samples of your own writing, such as emails or notes

## Is it okay to use AI for a reference or recommendation letter?

**It's a gray area rather than a settled rule, so check with the person or program asking for the letter.** A [GradPilot audit](https://gradpilot.com/news/letters-of-recommendation-ai-blind-spot) of 174 U.S. university AI policies found zero that name "letters of recommendation" in their formal scope. That's a third-party vendor analysis, not an official source, and individual programs may set their own expectations.

The safer practice is the one that holds either way: use AI to draft and organize, supply the facts yourself, and only sign what you'd stand behind.

Honesty matters beyond etiquette, though the legal sources below are about employers giving references, not individuals writing personal letters. In the U.S., there's no comprehensive federal prohibition on giving references, but [employers must avoid defamation, harassment and retaliation](https://thelegalguide.org/what-employers-can-legally-disclose-former-employees/), and state rules vary. In California, for instance, [references given without malice are protected from defamation claims](https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/california-reference-law.html), but an employer can be sued if it knows its statements are false or acts with reckless disregard for their truth. Laws differ by state, so don't treat any one rule as universal. This is general information, not legal advice; if you have a specific legal concern, talk to a qualified professional.

## FAQ

### What should a reference letter include?

How you know the person, how long you've known them, specific examples of their character or work, a clear statement of endorsement, and your contact details so the reader can follow up.

### Is a reference letter the same as a recommendation letter?

No. A reference letter is a general endorsement of someone's character, knowledge and skills, while a recommendation letter supports one specific scholarship, program or job.

### How do I stop an AI reference letter from sounding generic?

Feed it real specifics (a project, a result, a moment you remember) and give it samples of your own writing to match. Avoid superlatives, and edit anything you can't personally vouch for.

### Can I use "To Whom It May Concern" on a reference letter?

Yes. Reference letters are often general and not addressed to a particular requestor, so they commonly open with "To Whom It May Concern" or "Dear Sir/Madam". If you know the recipient, use their name.

### Do universities allow AI-written recommendation letters?

It's unsettled. One vendor audit of 174 U.S. university AI policies found none that name recommendation letters in their formal scope, but that is a third-party count and individual programs may have their own rules. Check with the program.
