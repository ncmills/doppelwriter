If you've ever pasted a draft into an AI tool and gotten back something that sounds like a press release, you already know the problem. The good news: several tools now let you feed in your own writing and steer the output toward it. The catch is that they work differently, and none of them is magic. Here's how the main options compare and what each one's documentation or coverage says about the setup.

## Which AI tools can learn my writing style?

**Grammarly, Claude, ChatGPT, and Jasper all offer some way to teach the tool your style, but each does it differently, and the exact menus and plan availability for some of them (Claude's style feature in particular) have shifted and should be checked against the current product.** Grammarly builds a voice profile from your writing, Claude has a custom style feature that works from a sample or a description, ChatGPT relies on pasted samples plus custom instructions and memory, and Jasper's Brand Voice has two parts: Memory and Tone & Style.

| Tool | How it learns your style | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Grammarly | Voice profile built from your writing, updated continuously as you write | Grammarly Support |
| Claude | Custom style from an uploaded or pasted writing sample, or a description of your style | How-To Geek; third-party guide |
| ChatGPT | Pasted samples, a style summary, and memory (custom instructions capped at 1,500 or 5,000 characters by plan) | OpenAI Academy; MemoryLake quoting OpenAI |
| Jasper | Brand Voice in two parts: Memory (facts about your business) and Tone & Style (rules for how Jasper writes) | Jasper launch post |
| DoppelWriter | You upload 3-5 writing samples, analyzed across 30+ dimensions (vendor claim) | DoppelWriter homepage |

## How does Grammarly's voice feature work?

**Grammarly builds a voice profile from each user's writing and lets you rewrite text in your own voice.** According to [Grammarly's support documentation](https://support.grammarly.com/hc/en-us/articles/23153676821773-Introducing-voice-features), the profile updates continuously as you write, and Grammarly keeps separate profiles for Documents and Messages, applying whichever fits the app or site you're in.

The documentation lists voice features as included in Pro, Plus, Business, and Enterprise plans. Plan details change, so check Grammarly's [features page](https://www.grammarly.com/features) before you buy; it lists tone adjustments, full-sentence rewrites, and 1,000 AI prompts per month under Pro, with brand tones and style guides under Business.

## How do I create a custom writing style in Claude?

**Claude's custom styles let you upload a writing sample or describe your style, but the exact menu path and plan availability may have changed, so check the current interface.** [How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/claude-ai-now-can-sample-and-mimic-your-writing-style/) reports that you can upload a sample or describe your style, and that the built-in presets are Normal, Concise, Explanatory, and Formal.

One [third-party guide](https://promptoptimizer.tools/blog/how-to-use-claude-styles) describes the path as "Use style" → "Create & edit styles" → "Create custom style" → "Add Writing Example," where you can upload a pdf, doc, or txt file or paste text. That is the guide's description as of its writing.

## Can ChatGPT write like me?

**Yes, if you give it real samples and a clear instruction to mirror them.** [OpenAI's guidance](https://academy.openai.com/public/clubs/work-users-ynjqu/resources/customizing-chatgpt) is to paste writing samples, ask ChatGPT to summarize your tone and style, then ask it to mirror that style. It also suggests turning on memory so style preferences carry across conversations.

A prompt that follows that approach:

```
Here are three emails I wrote. Describe my tone, sentence length,
vocabulary, and any habits you notice (openers, sign-offs, punctuation).
Then write a reply to the message below in that style.
```

A third-party source, [MemoryLake](https://www.memorylake.ai/en/blogs/teach-chatgpt-your-writing-style), covers using custom instructions for this and quotes OpenAI's limits: up to 1,500 characters on Free and Go and up to 5,000 on Plus, Pro, Enterprise, Business, and Education. A long style guide won't fit, so a short summary of your main habits is the practical option.

## What about Jasper's Brand Voice?

**Jasper describes Brand Voice as two parts: Memory and Tone & Style.** According to [Jasper's launch post](https://www.jasper.ai/blog/introducing-brand-voice), Memory holds facts about your business, and Tone & Style holds rules for how Jasper writes. A [third-party article](https://dynamicbusiness.com/ai-tools/jasper-brand-voice-manager-consistent-brand-messaging.html) reports that a voice can be trained with up to eight samples (text, URLs, or documents) and that workspace admins can set one voice as the default; we haven't verified those details on Jasper's own pages.

## Are general AI tools or dedicated voice tools better for matching style?

**General tools can get you close on tone; matching sentence-level structure is harder.** One [developer experiment](https://dev.to/thatechmaestro/replicate-an-authors-writing-style-using-prompt-engineering-insights-from-an-experiment-with-2hfk) found Claude's custom style captured tone and theme but not the sentence-level structure of the author being imitated. Pairing a real sample with a specific rewrite prompt improved results for both Claude and GPT-4o. That's a single anecdotal test, not a benchmark.

## How do I get better results from any of these tools?

**The one result with experimental support is that a real sample plus a specific rewrite prompt improved output for both Claude and GPT-4o.** Beyond that, a few common-sense habits are worth trying:

- **Start from real samples.** The developer experiment above found they improved results.
- **Describe the style before you apply it.** Ask the model to name your habits, then correct anything it gets wrong.
- **Compare against your own writing.** Read the draft next to a real sample and fix what doesn't sound like you.

## FAQ

### Can ChatGPT write in my style?

Yes, with some setup. OpenAI's guidance is to paste writing samples, have ChatGPT summarize your tone and style, then ask it to mirror that style. Custom instructions are capped at 1,500 characters on Free and Go plans and 5,000 on paid plans, so keep any style summary short.

### Does Claude need a certain number of writing samples?

No official figure was available to us. A third-party guide suggests a few blog posts as a starting point, but treat that as a rule of thumb rather than a requirement.

### Does ChatGPT's memory learn my voice automatically?

Not reliably. OpenAI suggests turning on memory so style preferences persist, but a third-party source argues memory summarizes your style rather than reproducing it. For important pieces, paste fresh samples.

### Can any AI tool match my voice perfectly?

No. We found no independent accuracy benchmark, only vendor claims and one anecdotal experiment. Always compare the output to your own writing and edit.
