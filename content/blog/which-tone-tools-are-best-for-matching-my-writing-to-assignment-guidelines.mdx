You have a prompt that says "formal academic tone" or "professional but approachable," and a draft that may or may not fit. Several tools claim to help. The honest summary: they each cover a different slice of "tone," and none of them reads your assignment sheet. Here's how to pick.

## Can any tone tool check my writing against my assignment guidelines?

**No tool in this comparison is documented to check your text against a specific rubric or assignment prompt.** I looked for that feature across Grammarly, ProWritingAid, Hemingway, QuillBot and DoppelWriter and found no source describing it. What these tools do is evaluate tone, style or readability in general terms. Your job is to translate the guidelines ("formal," "third person," "undergraduate reading level") into settings or checks the tool can actually perform.

## Which tone tool is best for an academic assignment?

**ProWritingAid is the strongest fit, because it has a built-in Academic style that changes how it judges your writing.** You can set a document's style to General, Academic, Business, Technical, Creative, Web or Script. According to [ProWritingAid](https://prowritingaid.com/art/1294/get-custom-writing-style-suggestions-with-prowritingaid.aspx), the Academic style treats passive voice as more acceptable and disables certain rules, such as the one that flags smileys.

A third-party review describes its Formality Report as assigning a formality score and flagging phrases that fall below the expected academic register ([AI Stack Picks](https://aistackpicks.com/reviews/prowritingaid-for-academic-writing-2026/)). The same reviewer cautions that its style suggestions can be wrong for technical fields such as chemistry, engineering and medical research. Treat it as a first pass, not a verdict.

## What does Grammarly's tone detector actually do?

**It labels the tone it detects in your writing; it doesn't compare that tone to a target.** [Grammarly](https://www.grammarly.com/tone) says it analyzes word choice, phrasing, punctuation and capitalization. [XDA Developers](https://www.xda-developers.com/grammarly-tone-detector-available-keyboard-app/) reports it can identify over 40 tones, including "formal," "informal," "confident" and "optimistic."

That's useful as a mirror: if your guidelines say "formal" and the detector says "informal," you've found something to revise. Per [TechHQ](https://techhq.com/news/grammarly-why-tone-is-integral-to-your-business-emails/), it only offers suggestions that you can accept or reject. One caveat: sources disagree on the minimum text length it needs (Grammarly's own mobile-keyboard post says 90 characters, others cite 120 or 150), so don't be surprised if very short passages return nothing.

## How do I check readability against a grade-level requirement?

**Use Hemingway Editor's free web version, which gives a grade-level assessment and color-coded highlights.** According to [Reedsy's review](https://reedsy.com/blog/guide/book-writing-software/hemingway-app-review/), it aims for Grade 9 by default. That's a sensible target for web copy and a poor one for a dense upper-level paper, so treat the score as a diagnostic rather than a goal. If your assignment specifies a reading level, use the number to see which direction you're off in.

The desktop app is a one-time $19.99 purchase with no AI features, per the same review. I didn't verify Hemingway's paid-plan pricing from an official page, so check it directly.

## Where does QuillBot fit?

**QuillBot is a rewriting tool, not an evaluator, so it helps after you've found a tone problem.** Per [SupWriter's review](https://supwriter.com/blog/quillbot-review-2026), the free plan paraphrases up to 125 words at a time in two modes (Standard and Fluency). Premium is $8.33/month billed annually and adds Academic and other modes, custom modes and tone insights.

The 125-word cap makes it awkward for whole essays on the free plan. Also remember that paraphrasing someone else's text is not the same as revising your own, and your course's rules on AI assistance still apply. I couldn't verify that any of these tools guarantees compliance with a specific policy.

## What if the guideline is "sound like you" rather than a generic tone?

**Then a style-profile tool is closer to what you need than a tone label.** DoppelWriter says you upload 3-5 writing samples and it analyzes style across 30+ dimensions, which is a vendor claim ([DoppelWriter](https://doppelwriter.com/blog/ai-tools-for-writing-styles)). It also offers a free Voice Analyzer that needs no signup. Its own site notes there is no independent accuracy benchmark for style matching, so judge it by whether the output reads like your earlier work.

A practical workflow for a "formal but personal" assignment:

1. Draft in your own words first.
2. Run Hemingway to check reading level against the guideline.
3. Run ProWritingAid with the Academic style, or Grammarly's tone detector, to check register.
4. If you want a voice-matched rewrite of a section, try a profile-based tool.

## So which tool should I choose?

**Match the tool to the thing your guideline actually specifies.**

| Guideline says | Start with |
|---|---|
| "Academic" or "formal" register | ProWritingAid (Academic style) |
| "Professional but friendly," email or cover letter tone | Grammarly tone detector |
| A reading level or "clear and concise" | Hemingway Editor |
| "Rephrase to sound more scholarly" | QuillBot (Premium for Academic mode) |
| "Write in your own voice" | A style-profile tool such as DoppelWriter |

Whatever you pick, reread the assignment sheet last. The tools flag patterns; only you can confirm the draft answers the prompt.

ProWritingAid, because its Academic style changes its rules (for example, treating passive voice as more acceptable). A reviewer cautions its suggestions can be wrong in technical fields.

Yes. Hemingway's free web version gives a grade-level assessment. Its Grade 9 default may be too low for upper-level academic work.

No. It only offers suggestions you can accept or reject.

I couldn't confirm one for any tool here, including DoppelWriter. Test it on your own samples.
